A first course, not a finish line.

My Cisco Introduction to Cybersecurity completion, kept in perspective. A small starting point for more learning and practice.

WHAT THIS NOTE IS BASED ONI completed Cisco Networking Academy's Introduction to Cybersecurity course on November 8, 2026. This page records that real milestone and a short security-basics reference. It is not a professional certification claim or a transcript of my course answers.

What is actually complete

The introductory course is complete, with a course-completion certificate and badge. That is the scope of this milestone. It does not make me an expert, prove practical mastery, or stand in for deeper work.

THE RIGHT SCALEA completed introduction is a starting point. The rest of the portfolio should show the practice and projects that come after it, without pretending they happened before they did.

A small security-basics reference

These are general reminders from public security guidance, not a claim about a specific course exercise or changes I have already made to all of my accounts.

  • Use a different strong password for each account. A password manager can help generate and store long, random, unique passwords.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication where it is available. It adds another check beyond a password; it does not remove every account risk.
  • Keep the distinction between knowing a term and applying it correctly. Notes, small projects, and authorized practice can make the gaps easier to see.

What belongs in a later note

A useful lab note needs a real task, what I observed, what I tried, what changed, and what remains unclear. I will keep course completion separate from platform-room or machine completion.

There is no HTB machine or TryHackMe room walkthrough on this page. Platform-specific write-ups should only be added when the work and the rules for sharing it are clear. No active HTB machine solutions are included here.
